01

The pool is never neutral

Bayesian reasoning shows how small early advantages compound: every positive signal raises the posterior probability of success — again and again, until early luck starts to look like talent.

No single signal decides anything. Together they compound — which is exactly why a screening model trained on "past successful hires" reads accumulated privilege as merit.

02

Rules manufacture "talent"

Canadian youth hockey groups children by calendar year, cut-off 1 January. At ten, eleven months is height, weight and strength. Scouts pick the visibly stronger — who get better coaching, more ice time, elite competition. The gap compounds until it looks like natural talent: the Matthew Effect, manufactured by a grouping rule.

The same logic runs inside résumé screening: "gap year" penalties, degree-tier filters and experience thresholds are all grouping rules — and the more “objective” they look, the easier it is to misread manufactured gaps as ability gaps.

03

Numbers can lie both ways

In 1973, Berkeley's graduate school admitted 44% of men and 35% of women — and the cold aggregate nearly branded the university discriminatory. Broken down by department, most admitted women at equal or higher rates: women had simply applied to the most competitive departments. Simpson's Paradox.

Interactive · Toggle the lens
Men Women

One aggregate number, one accusation. Flip the view to see what it hid.

The numbers were never just numbers: applicants experience the aggregate as a verdict on their lives. Presentation is part of fairness — stratified rates, base rates, and plain-language context.

04

Governance by design: warmth in automation

If the gap cannot be fully eliminated, governance must intervene — not with one grand fix, but at three complementary levels.

In 2024, a deaf Native American applicant alleged that HireVue garbled her answers, the employer refused her requested captioning, and the interview closed with an AI verdict to “improve communication skills”. The system did not merely miss her disability — it delivered a cold judgement that wounded her dignity.

Automated decisions are experienced as person-to-person verdicts. Warmth is not soft decoration.

🛠️

Technical

  • Captions and alternative assessment channels
  • Human review for disabled and accented candidates
  • Fairness audits of proxies and thresholds
💬

Expressive

  • Publish stratified rates with base rates and uncertainty
  • Plain-language explanation of what the number does — and does not — mean
  • Rejection reasons that preserve self-respect: truthful without cruelty
🏛️

Institutional

  • Accommodation request processes
  • Appeal routes backed by human review
  • Transparent, regularly published audits

This is not only fairness but human flourishing: giving those without early preparation a stage to shine, and protecting the vulnerable from careless harm. Progress is not only technological — it is also the inclusion between people.
